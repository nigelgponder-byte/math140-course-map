# Research & Evidence Repository

This folder houses empirical citations, ethical risk assessments, stakeholder impact matrices, and baseline comparisons for the AI intervention in **MATH 140**.

## Core Empirical Literature

1. **Bastani et al. (PNAS 2025)** – *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics*
   - **Key Finding:** Nearly 1,000 high school math students. Unguarded GPT-4 raised practice grades by 48%, but once access was removed those students scored 17% lower on exams than students who never had it. A safeguarded tutor raised practice grades by 127%, and its safeguards largely mitigated the exam harm.
   - **Implication:** The tool must remain strictly hint-only and never supply statements or reasons.

2. **Kestin et al. (Scientific Reports 2025)** – *AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning*
   - **Key Finding:** In a randomized trial with 194 Harvard physics students, a purpose-built AI tutor following pedagogical best practices produced median learning gains more than double those of an in-class active-learning lesson, in less time. Students also felt more engaged and more motivated.
   - **Implication:** The tutor ends each response with a single Socratic question forcing student justification.

3. **Petrov et al. (arXiv 2025)** – *Proof or Bluff? Evaluating LLMs on 2025 USA Math Olympiad*
   - **Key Finding:** Experts graded AI proofs on the 2025 USA Math Olympiad. The best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) scored 25% and all others scored under 5%, yet the models presented their proofs confidently. This is a preprint, and Olympiad proofs are far harder than Math 140 proofs.
   - **Implication:** The tool cannot be trusted to grade or certify mathematical correctness; final evaluation must remain strictly human.
